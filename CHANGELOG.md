# Changelog

## Unreleased (1.0)

- Your Place · Data stands on its own: its own address, start page, title, link preview and install icon, and nothing to print. Open data about a city on a 3D model, one layer at a time, so the model doesn't get crowded. Share a view as a link (a shared view invites people to look at their own city) or download it as an image with your own title and the data's credits.
- Steepness: roads and paths coloured by how steep they are, from golden yellow (3–6 %) through orange and red to dark red (over 15 %), anywhere, from the terrain the model already has. Planning a run, a ride, a hike or a stroll? This is where it climbs.
- Your route: load a GPX or TCX file (Strava, Garmin, a race website) from the start page, the panel or by dropping it on the page. A course longer than the model shows the stretch you pick, the finish first; the line runs on the terrain in the steepness colours with start, finish and km markers and its three steepest climbs labelled ("+9 % · 400 m"); a height profile shows metres up and down, the steepest point and what's under your finger; a replay moves along it at the recorded pace with the race clock. Hide the first and last 500 m where a run from home starts and ends. The file is read in the browser and never uploaded or stored; share links include the route only if you tick that. Suggested on Reddit.
- Traffic in Hamburg: how many cars or bikes the city's counting points counted in each of the last 24 hours, live from Hamburg's open data, as red streets that rise with the traffic, and a bar chart of the day. Each count spreads along the streets around its counting point and fades out over the distance you set (100–500 m); where counts meet, as at a crossing, they add up, so the crossing rises as a whole instead of a spike at each point. Only the white dots are counted; in between it's an estimate. Pick an hour in the chart or replay the day and the streets follow; tap a dot for that point's day; the chart is also a table. On screen a route rides on top of them in dark ink. Only Hamburg so far; elsewhere the panel says so and offers Hamburg.
- Public transport from your city's timetable: load the GTFS file your transit operator publishes (from the start page, the panel, or by dropping the ZIP on the page) and trams, buses, trains, U-Bahn and ferries move through the square as the timetable has them. The same panel as Traffic: trips into the square per hour as a bar chart (and a table), a clock, a replay at 1 minute, 10 minutes or 1 hour a second, any day the timetable has, and the lines as chips; tap a vehicle or a chip to see one line. "Find timetables for this place" lists the open timetables the Mobility Database has for the square, most local first, with their size, and loads one in a tap; more can be added, as a city's buses and trains often come separately, and each is credited with its licence. A file of your own works too. Files are read in the browser, in the background, and never uploaded; a city's file works, Hamburg's 39 MB one and Berlin's 85 MB one too; Berlin's U-Bahn shows as U-Bahn. If the square has none of its lines, the app goes where it's busiest. Replays at 10 minutes or an hour a second leave fading trails behind the vehicles, like a long-exposure photo. Try it with Freiburg's timetable, which we host.
- Homes and Income: the buildings coloured by what homes cost, or what people earn, in the area they stand in, from open data you bring, as with timetables. The panel lists the open sources known for the place: Germany's 2022 census rents per m² in 100 m squares (download the ZIP, load it as it is), CBS's home values and incomes for the Netherlands (one tap, for the square), and Census Reporter's median home values and household incomes for US block groups. Any GeoJSON or CSV with values per area works too, grid squares, areas or single points (shown only as medians of 100 m squares with at least five). Five blues each hold a fifth of the areas in the square; the pin's area is outlined, with its value and how it compares. Files are read in the browser and never uploaded. Try Freiburg's rents or Amsterdam's incomes, which we host.
- A play button in the bar at the bottom, next to the place's name, whenever something moves in time (the timetable, the traffic counts or your route): it plays and pauses with the panel closed, and shows the time (or how far along the route).
- Your route is no longer one of the layers: it has its own place under the layer switch and on the start page, and shows over any layer. On Steepness only the route is coloured.
- The first layers of the new data-layer engine (`src/data/`): sources, matching to the model, time and playback, and displays, ready for more open data.

## Before 1.0

Your Place · Data grew out of [Your Place](https://yourplace3d.github.io/3d-city/) 1.7.0 (a 3D model of any address, as a poster or a 3D print), whose search, terrain, buildings, map layers, pin, share links and languages it shares. Your Place's own history is in its changelog on the `main` branch.
