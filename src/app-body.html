    Data layer

      Roads and paths under 3% stay as they are. Estimated from terrain data with points 10–30 m apart, so short ramps can be missed; bridges are left out.

      Less trafficMore
      300 m

      Streets near a counting point (the white dots) rise and turn red with the traffic in the hour you pick. Each count spreads along its street and fades out over the distance you set; where counts meet, as at a crossing, they add up. Only the dots are counted: in between it’s an estimate. Tap a dot to see its day. The last 24 hours, Hamburg time.

      Traffic counts: Freie und Hansestadt Hamburg, Behörde für Verkehr und Mobilitätswende (dl-de/by-2-0), live from iot.hamburg.de.

      Transit operators publish their timetables as GTFS files (ZIP). Look for your city’s on its operator’s or open data website, or at transit.land or mobilitydatabase.org. The file stays on this device. It’s never uploaded.

        Vehicles move as the timetable plans them, not live: delays and cancellations don’t show. Tap a vehicle or a line to see that line on its own. U-Bahn and metro lines are dashed and their trains see-through, as most run underground.

        This file has no line shapes, so lines run straight from stop to stop.
